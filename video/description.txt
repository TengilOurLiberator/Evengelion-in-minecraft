I GOT IN THE ROBOT: Evangelion episode 1, rebuilt as a Minecraft VR game where the AI characters talk back

I play a 14-year-old boy summoned to a city under attack. Everything the other characters say is spoken live by AI, they hear what I say through my microphone, and they answer in character. Then I get in the giant robot and fight.

This is an unofficial fan project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by khara, Gainax, or anyone who owns Neon Genesis Evangelion. No audio, footage, music or dialogue from the series is used: the script, voices, sound and score were all made new for this.

HOW IT WAS MADE

The whole thing was built with Claude Code in about three days. One Claude model ran the project as lead; it wrote the script and the staging and handed the building to a team of cheaper Claude agents, up to a dozen at a time, each owning one piece: the sets, the story engine, the characters, the monster, the sound, the testing.

The game
- Minecraft Java 26.3 with Fabric, played in a Meta Quest through Vivecraft.
- No mods add content. Everything is a vanilla datapack and a resource pack.
- The world is an empty void. Four sets (the seaside town, the night city, the underground cavern, the headquarters hangar) are generated by Python as about 8 million blocks and stamped into the world in half a minute.
- The story is a state machine compiled from Python into thousands of Minecraft functions. It never runs on a timer you can lose to: each beat starts when you arrive somewhere or say something.
- Misato's car, the aircraft and the rail wagon are custom 3D models. The monster is 133 moving display parts. Unit 01's head is a block sculpture.
- In the fight I am scaled up 16 times with Minecraft's own scale attribute, and VR scales my real room with it.

The voices
- A Python program listens to my microphone (Silero VAD, Whisper), works out who I am talking to, and asks Claude for that character's reply using a written character sheet and what has happened in the story so far.
- The reply is spoken with Chatterbox text-to-speech in that character's voice, usually within two or three seconds.
- The scripted lines are performed the same way, live, and about half are lightly re-voiced each run to fit what I actually said. So no two playthroughs are word for word the same.
- When the story needs an answer from me, a "Your turn" sign appears. At the big decision it listens for whether I say yes.
- No voice from the series or its actors was used or imitated as a reference.

Sound and music
- About 100 sound effects, ambience loops and music cues. Some are synthesised from code, the rest generated with local audio models (ACE-Step and Stable Audio). None of it is from the show.

Testing
- Before I ever put the headset on, an automated player ran the whole story on a test server while a second program took hundreds of in-game screenshots, and the AI lead judged them and sent fixes back. Then I played it, broke it in new ways, and we fixed those too.

What I did
- I had the idea, directed it, recorded the first version of every voice line myself, playtested in the headset, and reported what was broken. And the commentary is all me.
